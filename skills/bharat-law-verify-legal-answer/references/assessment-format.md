# Assessment format

Start with the material conclusion and the limits of the check. State the relevant jurisdiction, legal date, and evidence coverage where they affect the result.

Use a compact table when several claims need comparison:

| Claim | Finding | Evidence and pinpoint | Correction or limitation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

Use these findings consistently:

- **Supported:** retrieved evidence supports the material proposition within the stated scope.
- **Partly supported:** some elements are supported, but material qualifications or exceptions are missing.
- **Contradicted:** retrieved evidence materially conflicts with the proposition; explain the conflict and any unresolved applicability issue.
- **Insufficient evidence:** available evidence does not resolve the claim. This includes unavailable text, incomplete coverage, or unknown treatment.

For citation-dependent findings, separate what is established about the authority's identity, quoted wording, proposition support, applicability, and treatment. A correct citation alone does not establish the remaining items. Do not describe historical validity or present treatment as established without evidence.

Then provide the corrected answer, preserving useful qualifications and citing the actual passages used. Include unresolved points and a focused next research step only where useful. Never invent a citation, pinpoint, source link, quotation, check time, or treatment result to complete the format.
