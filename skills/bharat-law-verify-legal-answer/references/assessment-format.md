# Assessment format

Start with the material conclusion and the limits of the check. State jurisdiction, legal date, passages examined, and retrieval time when returned and relevant.

For each judgment or statute mention, separate identity/existence from content:

| Mention | Identity/existence | Key aspect or quotation | Content finding | Evidence and pinpoint | Correction or limitation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Use **matched**, **ambiguous**, or **no matching authority found** for identity. A limited or failed search does not prove nonexistence. Use these findings for content claims:

- **Supported:** examined source text supports the material proposition within the stated scope.
- **Partly supported:** some elements are supported, but material qualifications or exceptions are missing.
- **Contradicted:** examined source text materially conflicts with the proposition; explain the conflict and unresolved applicability issues.
- **Insufficient evidence:** available text does not resolve the claim, including missing text, unavailable original judgment wording, incomplete coverage, and unknown treatment.

Name the evidence basis: judgment details/headnotes, complete section text, available Act/component text, or description only. Key aspects may be supported by headnotes, with that limitation explicit. Headnotes cannot verify an exact quotation from the original judgment; mark it insufficient evidence. Complete section text or other full statute text may support quotation checks within the returned coverage. A title or description alone establishes neither detailed content nor current legal treatment. Do not assume complete original-document coverage or historical validity from a match alone.

Then provide the corrected answer only as far as the evidence permits, citing the actual source URLs and passages used. Never invent a citation, pinpoint, link, quotation, check time, or treatment result to complete the format.
